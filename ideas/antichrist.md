## 1. Antichrist, Beast, False Prophet, and the Man of Sin

The Bible uses several different terms and images for the end-time evil powers.  
People often merge them into one single figure called “the Antichrist,” but the Scriptures are more precise and nuanced.

### Key Facts from the Text
- **The word “Antichrist”** appears **only four times**, and **only in the letters of John**:
  - 1 John 2:18 → “antichrist is coming” but “many antichrists have come already.”
  - 1 John 2:22 → “This is the antichrist, he who denies the Father and the Son.”
  - 1 John 4:3 → “This is the spirit of the antichrist, which you heard was coming and now is in the world already.”
  - 2 John 7 → “Such a one is the deceiver and the antichrist.”

  → John defines the antichrist primarily as a **spirit of denial** already at work, not necessarily one future man.

- **The Beast and the False Prophet** (Revelation 13 & 19)
  - Revelation 13 describes two beasts: one from the sea (political/systemic power) and one from the earth (religious/false prophet system).
  - In Revelation 19:19-20 they are captured and **thrown alive into the lake of fire**.
  - This language sounds personal (“these two were thrown alive”), yet the whole book uses heavy symbolism.  
    → Many readers see them as **both a system and its final personal leaders**.

- **The Man of Sin / Son of Perdition** (2 Thessalonians 2:3-4)
  - Paul describes “the man of lawlessness, the son of destruction” who:
    - Opposes and exalts himself above every so-called god.
    - **Takes his seat in the temple of God**, proclaiming himself to be God.
  - This figure clearly draws from Daniel 7 (the little horn / king who exalts himself) and Daniel 11.

**Important observation**:  
Nowhere does the Bible explicitly call the Man of Sin, the Beast, or the False Prophet “the Antichrist.”  
The popular idea that “the one who sits in the temple = the Antichrist” is a later interpretation that merges several descriptions. The Bible itself keeps them distinct while showing they work together in the end.
